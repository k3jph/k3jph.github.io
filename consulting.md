Some problems are clean enough to belong to one field. The interesting ones
usually are not.

I take on consulting work where mathematics, statistics, software, artificial
intelligence, or public policy have to produce an answer somebody can actually
use. That may mean building a model, reviewing one, making sense of ugly data,
designing an analytical system, estimating risk, comparing decisions, or
explaining why a seemingly precise result does not answer the question everyone
thought it did.

I am particularly useful when the problem crosses boundaries. A forecasting
problem can become a data-engineering problem. A software system can become a
governance problem. A policy question can collapse into an argument about
assumptions. This is normal.

## What I Can Help With

### Analysis and Decision Support

Statistical modeling and inference; forecasting and simulation; risk and
uncertainty; operations research; program evaluation; benefit-cost and
cost-effectiveness analysis; quantitative policy analysis; and decision support.

### Artificial Intelligence and Analytical Systems

Applied artificial intelligence and machine learning; model evaluation;
model-management and model-serving workflows; analytical system architecture;
and the less glamorous work required to turn a model into something an
organization can actually use.

### Software and Scientific Computing

Research software, analytical tooling, data processing, prototypes, numerical
methods, and the occasional program that exists because I needed it and it
wasn't there.

### Independent Technical Review

Sometimes the useful answer is not another model. I also review analyses,
assumptions, methods, code, and technical claims, especially where the result
will be used to make an operational, policy, or technical decision.

## What the Work Looks Like

Most consulting problems start less neatly than the headings above. The data are
incomplete. The question is slightly wrong. The system already exists. Several
people mean different things by the same word. A useful engagement starts by
figuring out what decision actually has to be made, then choosing enough
mathematics, software, or analysis to support it.

The result might be code, a model, a technical review, a decision memo, a
prototype, or simply a defensible answer to a question that was harder than it
first appeared.
